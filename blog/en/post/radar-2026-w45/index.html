e-Müstahsil deadline moves to June 2027, UiPath merges agent governance on 2 December

Issue five: more time for SMS approval of e-Müstahsil receipts, Türkiye’s electronic informed-consent rules, UiPath coded-agent governance, IXP and validation in Maestro Flow, Autopilot in Orchestrator, Gartner.

Weekly Radar·Governance & operations·November 2, 2026·7 min read·Türkçesini okuyun

This week is about how approvals and records are kept. Türkiye’s revenue administration gave taxpayers eight more months to replace wet signatures on producer receipts with SMS verification, and the Ministry of Health set out the rules for taking patient consent the same way, with a one-time SMS code. On the platform side, UiPath is putting every agent under one governance policy from 2 December.

GİB: deadline for SMS verification of e-Müstahsil receipts extended to 30 June 2027

Turkish Revenue Administration · 28 October 2026

On 22 May 2026 GİB had announced that the changes in its e-Müstahsil Makbuzu technical guide would be mandatory for every receipt issued from 5 November 2026. The e-Müstahsil Makbuzu is the electronic receipt issued when buying produce from a farmer, and the changes replace the farmer’s wet signature with verification through IT or communication channels. According to the 28 October announcement, GİB reviewed feedback from taxpayers and extended the deadline to 30 June 2027. Taxpayers who have finished the work can use the new method before then. Last week I wrote that I couldn’t find an effective date for this change in the e-Archive guide; it was in the May announcement, and it has now moved.

Why it matters. A change that would have been mandatory this Thursday was pushed back at the last minute. That’s breathing room for teams who squeezed their project to hit 5 November, and a warning for those who haven’t started. My suggestion is to keep the scope and relax the schedule rather than let the work drift. Redesign the receipt flow around SMS approval and go live in a quiet period, outside harvest peaks, where the risk is lower. If you rely on an integrator, ask for a release plan against the new date. And if you already deployed a flow for 5 November that isn’t in use yet, don’t let it go stale.

Ministry of Health: informed consent can now be taken electronically with an SMS code

Official Gazette of Türkiye · 27 October 2026 (Issue 33413)

The Ministry of Health’s regulation sets out how informed consent before a medical intervention can be taken electronically, how the form reaches the patient and how the consent is recorded. It covers primary, secondary and tertiary health facilities. Consent is collected through e-Nabız, the national personal health record, or through the facility’s communication tool linked to its health information system. The patient confirms by entering a one-time SMS code, sent to a verified mobile number, into e-Nabız or by giving it to the health professional. Consent records and audit trails follow the same retention and destruction periods as paper forms, and they’re kept with an integrity value and a timestamp so they can be used in judicial and administrative reviews. If a legal representative is missing or can’t be verified electronically, consent can’t be taken electronically. The regulation refers to Türkiye’s data protection law (KVKK) and its Cyber Security Law, and took effect on publication.

Why it matters. In hospitals and insurers, consent forms are one of the paper jobs most often handed to automation: scanning, archiving, chasing missing signatures. This regulation moves much of that work to digital at the source. My reading: the value isn’t in scanning the form any more, it’s in the record set. The regulation requires the audit trail to hold at least the date and time, the SMS code details and the health professional’s details. If a robot in your estate creates or queries consents, check whether it writes to that trail too. One limit: when a representative can’t be verified, the paper process lives on, so you’ll be designing two flows, not one.

UiPath: coded and low-code agents move under the same governance policy on 2 December

UiPath Documentation · 29 October 2026

UiPath announced that from 2 December 2026 the AI Trust Layer will govern coded and low-code agents as one product. According to the Automation Cloud notes, coded agents will then use the LLM configurations and AI Trust Layer agent settings defined for low-code agents. Coded-agent settings will be hidden and no longer enforced. Existing coded agents need no code changes or redeployment, and pricing, licensing and SKUs stay the same. UiPath still warns that the change can break coded agents where the two sets of settings differ. Its advice is to review the agent settings in each policy and re-create any coded-agent-only LLM configurations under agents.

Why it matters. Two settings sets meant two audit questions: which agent uses which model, with which guardrail? One policy makes that simpler. The risky part is the switch itself. A model or logging setting a developer team chose for its coded agent could quietly revert to the low-code setting on 2 December. It’s the same kind of change as the audit-logging default and the Sonnet 4 swap we covered in October. This month, put the two lists side by side: coded-agent settings and agent settings. Where they differ, make the decision yourself before 2 December.

Maestro Flow: IXP extraction models built on the canvas, plus a human validation node

UiPath Documentation · 30 October 2026

According to the 30 October notes, the Extract node in Maestro Flow can now create an IXP model directly on the canvas, and that model can be reused across flows and solutions. A new Validate extraction node in the Human category lets a person check and correct the extracted data; it only works with Extract nodes created in the same flow. Both are in preview. The same release lets a run continue on a Quick Form node’s error path when the node fails.

Why it matters. In document processing, the slow part is usually the flow around the model rather than the model itself: exceptions, the validation queue, corrected data flowing back. Having those on one canvas simplifies the design. My reservation is reuse. If one IXP model serves several flows, a change to the model touches all of them. Setting a versioning and ownership rule when the model is first shared is far cheaper than adding one later. While it’s in preview I wouldn’t move production documents here. For a team weighing a move from Document Understanding to IXP, though, it’s a useful place to experiment.

Orchestrator: Autopilot Chat is back and can connect to MCP servers

UiPath Documentation · 28 October 2026

Autopilot Chat is available in Orchestrator again. It now works directly against the tenant to manage processes, jobs, queues, assets, triggers, machines and folders, investigate failed jobs and access problems, and help plan, build and deploy automations. Users choose an approval mode. In settings you can switch tools and skills on or off, connect MCP servers and add custom instructions. The same release lets Job recording capture both failed jobs and failed queue transactions for the same process.

Why it matters. Digging through logs to find why a job failed takes up a large share of an operations team’s day, so handing that to a chat interface is tempting. But here the chat tool acts like an administrator that can change assets and triggers. I think the first question is the approval mode: in a production tenant, write actions shouldn’t happen without human approval. The second is MCP: every server you connect opens a new area of access for Autopilot. Before switching it on in production, write down which role can reach which tools.

Gartner: 30% of employees replaced by AI may be rehired by 2029

Gartner · 28 October 2026

According to the title of Gartner’s 28 October release, 30% of employees replaced by AI will be rehired by 2029 if organisations don’t overhaul their workforce planning. Gartner’s page blocks automated access, so this item rests on the release title alone; I couldn’t read the basis for the prediction or the recommendations on the primary page, so I’ve left them out.

Why it matters. Most automation business cases book released capacity as headcount savings. The title points at the catch: turning capacity into a number before you’re sure the work has really gone tends to come back. My rule is to make headcount decisions only after the automation has run live, exceptions included, for at least one full cycle. Exception handling, validation and robot maintenance are someone’s job too; if they’re missing from the business case, the savings figure is incomplete.

Next week I’ll watch whether UiPath publishes further guidance for the 2 December switch, and whether GİB updates the e-Müstahsil guide to match the new date.
